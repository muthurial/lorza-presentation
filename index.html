02 / THE CONSEQUENTIAL UNKNOWN

What if you knew which question could change your case?

Lorza is designed to flag what is missing before you advise a client to settle, plead or proceed.

THE QUESTION WORTH ASKING

Find the evidence most likely to change your next decision.

What makes an inquiry worth pursuing?

Candidate objective: Maximise the expected value of the next defensible decision.

Start with the decision and competing explanations. Compare what a source could resolve against its reliability, time, cost, privacy and lawful availability. Account for the harm of being wrong. Record why a request was made, deferred or deliberately left unmade.

03 / THE INVESTIGATIVE METHOD

One place to run the investigation.

Lorza is an investigative workspace in development. It would keep evidence, questions, permissions and decisions together. Lawyers direct the work and choose where approved AI can help.

Proposed capability

01 Know what to investigate next

Use an authorised inquiry charter to record the question, rivals and decision criteria. Rank candidate evidence requests by likely decision value and record why lower-value retrieval was deferred.

02 Keep competing explanations alive

Link sources to claims and distinguish corroboration, contradiction and shared origins. Record unresolved conflicts and the conditions that would reopen an inquiry.

03 Preserve what was known, and when

Freeze predictions, selection rationale and evidence versions before reveal. Append later evidence and corrections as new entries. Keep proposals distinct from findings accepted by authorised lawyers.

04 Bring in expertise under defined terms

Give each approved human or automated contributor a specific question, evidence scope and submission permission. Keep the contribution attributable and reviewable.

05 Carry reasoning into legal work

Connect accepted findings and open questions to disclosure, witness preparation and argument. Let the lawyer decide when evidence is sufficient and when to reopen the inquiry.

ILLUSTRATIVE LORZA RECOMMENDATION

What independent evidence establishes that an actual loss occurred?

To establish an actual loss, trace the claim back to records that can independently confirm what moved.

Inspect the next evidence requests
  1. Reconcile physical cash and stock against independently confirmed remittances. Compare signed count sheets, carrier receipts and settlement records for the disputed periods. Agreement could support a real loss. A mismatch could point to a recording or transfer issue.
  2. Request targeted transaction and incident records. Check relevant dates, systems and coverage against independent records. This could distinguish posting error from physical loss. A filtered extract limits what can be inferred.
  3. Defer a broad search of staff records. Establish a loss and narrow the time window first, unless preservation or legal obligations require earlier action.

Possible branches include actual loss, accounting or system error, and a combination. These are retrospective test hypotheses. The recommendation cannot establish that any proposed record exists or could have been obtained.

Experts dispute the relevance of a known fault.

The defence raised the Callendar Square fault and other technical explanations. Prosecution witness Gareth Jenkins ruled out that fault as the cause. The trial had resumed on 11 October after earlier disclosure disputes.

Still open. Were the extracts complete enough to test the branch-specific hypotheses? Independent corroboration remained a separate question.

23 APRIL 2021 / LATER JUDICIAL FINDINGS

The convictions are quashed.

Misra was convicted of theft on 21 October 2010, after earlier false-accounting pleas. The Court of Appeal quashed all the convictions in 2021. It identified incomplete, filtered audit data and found no proof of an actual loss distinct from the Horizon shortage.

Later disclosure finding. The receipts and payments mismatch fault concerned Horizon Online, a different version. The court recorded that it should have been disclosed for its bearing on reliability. It did not identify that fault as the cause at West Byfleet.

Append the outcome. The earlier snapshots exclude this finding. A retrospective walkthrough cannot establish what a prospective trial would have achieved.

Appeal, §§198 and 206–209

What did retrieval cost in this case?

The prosecution’s 2010 submission said obtaining logs for December 2006 to December 2007 cost over £20,000 and yielded 431,490 transactions. This was the prosecution’s account, not a judicial finding about cost. The scale illustrates why period, coverage and the question being tested matter when defining a request.

Prosecution submission, POL00054346, §7

Why the records give different amounts

The February report gives the approximate audit shortage and the investigator’s precise assessment of actual loss. Computer Weekly described the charge as £74,000 in November 2009. The 2021 appeal judgment records £74,609.84 as the alleged shortfall and amount in the theft count. The sources do not provide a line-by-line reconciliation.

A council for a consequential decision

  1. Authorise a snapshot. Send M-17 only to eligible agents.
  2. Assign independent reviews. One agent traces corroboration, another tests alternative explanations.
  3. Freeze initial analyses. Preserve each rationale before comparison.

  7. Obtain lawyer authorisation. Counsel decides whether to proceed.

08 / ONE CASE. MANY MINDS. NO LOST THREADS.

Never lose sight of the questions that matter.

Lorza is designed to keep open questions visible, suggest which are worth asking next and explain why. New evidence can bring an earlier question back into view.

Illustrative workflow · disputed payment

01 Define the decision

Set the issue and permissions. Preserve competing explanations such as payment, misallocation or an incomplete ledger.

02 Test the evidence gap

Link each claim to its source. Compare targeted bank confirmation with a broader disclosure request and log the lawyer’s decision.

03 Bring in a scoped expert

Ask a forensic accountant to reconcile the selected transactions. Preserve the original evidence, the expert’s assumptions and their independent opinion.

04 Reassess and prepare work product

Compare the result with the frozen rationale. Approve supported findings and link them to a chronology, witness plan or draft argument. Keep unresolved issues visible. Agree when to pause and what new evidence would trigger a reassessment. Keep the original rationale alongside any revised inquiry plan.

Inspect the confidentiality boundary

Application services would check deterministic permissions for matter, branch and evidence before retrieval or export. A model gateway would send only the authorised snapshot to an approved processor and record scope, provider and policy. Private investigative memory would stay within each matter.

Uploaded text could supply evidence or contain a proposed change. It could not grant access, change tool permissions or alter the inquiry charter. Authorised changes would require review and a versioned record.

Isolation, revocation, logging, backups and external provider processing require implementation and independent validation. This presentation illustrates the intended controls.

Six acceptance gates before reliance

Proposed engineering and research requirements. These tests have not been executed by this presentation.

  1. Cross-matter isolation. Test that unauthorised identities cannot retrieve or infer another matter’s private state through search, tools or exports.
  2. AI non-interference. Test that model output and hostile source text cannot bypass permissions, alter frozen history or take consequential action without approval.
  3. Temporal record integrity. Replay a dated snapshot and verify its evidence, rationale and authorisations after later revisions.
  4. External-provider unlinkability. Define what the provider can observe, including identifiers, content and timing. Test which requests or matters can be linked under that threat model and record the limits.
  5. Administrator confidentiality. Specify the deployment, keys, logging and privileged access paths. Test what each operator role can read and audit exceptional access.
  6. Prospective investigative value. Predefine decisions and criteria, then compare against credible practitioner and model baselines using equal evidence cutoffs. Measure decision quality and investigation cost.
